Zooble hated new places. Not because they were dangerous—although, statistically speaking, they usually were—but because every new place came with a new set of rules nobody bothered to explain. The Underground apparently had magic. Wonderful. "So..." Zooble muttered, looking down at a glowing river. "Is the water supposed to be blue because it's magical, or because someone spilled approximately six million gallons of food coloring?" A tiny frog monster looked up. "...Magic." "Called it." Before anyone could elaborate, something whistled through the air. Zooble instinctively detached an arm. A spear embedded itself in the cave wall exactly where their head had been. "...Rude." A booming laugh echoed through the cavern. "HA! Nice dodge!" A tall monster in silver armor landed several feet away with enough force to make the rocks shake. Her single visible eye gleamed with excitement. "You've got reflexes!" Zooble looked at the spear. "I also have a very healthy fear of being impaled." "I'm Undyne!" "I'm aware you're loud." Undyne blinked. "...That's not usually people's first observation." "It should be." For a moment they simply stared at each other. Undyne's grin widened. "I like your attitude!" "I wouldn't call it an attitude." "What would you call it?" "A coping mechanism." Undyne decided, without consulting anyone involved, that Zooble was now her responsibility. "This is Waterfall!" "Water." "And... a fall." "...You're surprisingly good at naming things." Undyne ignored the sarcasm entirely. "And over here are echo flowers! They repeat whatever they hear!" One of the flowers immediately shouted: "WHATEVER THEY HEAR!" Another flower answered from somewhere deeper in the cavern. "WHATEVER THEY HEAR!" Zooble sighed. "I've been here six minutes and the local plant life is already louder than the circus." "The what?" "...Long story." "I LOVE long stories!" "I don't." Undyne had a talent for dragging people into activities. "Training!" "No." "Running!" "No." "Sparring!" "No." "Extreme cliff climbing!" "...How extreme?" "The cliff usually wins." "No." "What do you like?" Zooble considered the question. "...Sitting." Undyne frowned. "That's not an activity." "It is if you're committed." Despite every complaint Zooble made—and there were many—they kept following Undyne around. Not intentionally. It just... happened. Undyne had a gravitational pull usually reserved for black holes and overly enthusiastic motivational speakers. Every day brought something new. One afternoon she challenged a waterfall to "flow harder." Another day she spent twenty minutes arguing with a training dummy because she was convinced it looked smug. "You know that's made of wood, right?" Zooble asked. "It knows what it did." "I don't think it does." "Exactly." They eventually found themselves sitting on a cliff overlooking a lake filled with glowing blue light. For nearly five whole minutes... Undyne didn't say anything. Zooble looked over suspiciously. "Are you sick?" "What?" "You're being quiet." "I'm appreciating the scenery." "...That's unsettling." Undyne laughed. "I can appreciate scenery!" "I didn't say you couldn't." "You implied it." "I implied it was unusual." "...Okay, maybe a little." Silence returned. It wasn't awkward. The Underground echoed softly around them, water dripping somewhere in the darkness. Zooble looked at the reflections dancing across the lake. "It's nice." "Told you." "I didn't say you were right." "You kind of did." "I absolutely did not." "You emotionally did." "I have no idea what that means." "Neither do I!" "You know," Undyne said eventually, "you're hard to read." Zooble stared ahead. "That's intentional." "Why?" "...Because if people know what you're thinking..." "They understand you?" "They expect things." Undyne was quiet. For once, she didn't rush to fill the silence. Instead she rested her elbows on her knees. "I used to think being strong meant never letting anyone see when I was scared." Zooble glanced sideways. "But then I met people who trusted me with their fears." Her voice had lost its usual theatrical energy. "And I realized courage isn't pretending nothing hurts." She smiled faintly. "It's deciding someone matters enough that you're willing to hurt for them." Zooble looked back toward the glowing water. "...That's either incredibly inspiring..." Undyne grinned. "...or?" "...or the sappiest thing I've ever heard." "Hah!" "I haven't decided which." "You'll get there." For reasons Zooble couldn't quite explain, they hoped they would.
